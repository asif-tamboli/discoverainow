What “good” actually means

CorrectnessNo invented requirements or fake APIs.
CoverageImportant risks, not just more test cases.
PrioritizationHigh-impact failures are separated from low-value noise.
Automation qualityReliable waits, clear assertions and maintainable structure.
Hallucination controlUncertainty is labeled rather than silently filled in.
Cleanup requiredHow much human correction is needed before use.

Try the same experiment yourself

Take one representative story from your product. Before asking any model for test cases, write down 5–10 risks you expect a strong tester to notice. Then use the same requirement with each model and compare misses, not output length.

You are reviewing the requirement below as a senior QA engineer. First identify: 1. ambiguous or missing rules, 2. high-risk edge cases, 3. boundary conditions, 4. state/time/concurrency risks, 5. assumptions you would NOT make without clarification. Then generate a prioritized test set. Do not invent business rules. Label assumptions explicitly. REQUIREMENT: [PASTE REQUIREMENT]
